About
Interested in AI that has to work in the real world.

I'm MD Rayhan, a junior undergraduate at Guizhou University, China, originally from Bangladesh.

My interests lie at the intersection of embedded AI, robotics and physical intelligence. I am particularly interested in perception, embodied AI and human-robot collaboration, especially when robots have to make decisions and interact with people in uncertain environments.

My long-term direction is toward robotics systems where intelligence is not just demonstrated in software, but deployed safely on physical machines — including medical and autonomous robotics.

MICROCHEMICAL JOURNAL · 2026 · VOL. 230

Ultrasensitive “signal-on” photoelectrochemical aptasensor based on self-assembled Cu₃(BTC)₂/TiO₂ interface for trace Pb²⁺ monitoring in real water samples

Di Yang · Jie Cai · Md Rayhan · Qiuping Fu · Lulu Xiong · Danping Chen · Zhenchao Wang · Bingqian Liu

The work presents a DNAzyme-driven photoelectrochemical aptasensor for trace lead-ion monitoring and integrates weighted least squares calibration to address heteroscedasticity and matrix interference in quantitative measurements.

My contribution: Methodology and software, including the statistical calibration component and computational workflow.

I have also joined the university robotics club and am continuing to explore research and engineering projects around embodied AI, safe robotics and human-robot interaction.

PROJECT AEGIS · ONGOING

Learning to build robots
that know when reality disagrees.

Project Aegis is my ongoing engineering project for developing practical skills in embodied AI and robotics. The focus is not simply making a model work, but understanding what a robotic system should trust, how it should respond to uncertainty, and what happens when the real world differs from its assumptions.

The project is evolving toward a complete physical-AI workflow spanning perception, decision-making, control, simulation and eventually hardware deployment.
